\begin{problem}
    Show that any function \( f:\mathbb{Q}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z} \) satisfies the following
    property:
    \[
        \exists a,b,c\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with } a<b<c \text{ such that } f\left(a\right),
        f\left(c\right)\leq f\left(b\right)
    \]
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
    If \( \exists a,b,c \in \mathbb{Q} \) such that \( a < b < c \) and \( f\left(a\right) =
    f\left(b\right) = f\left(c\right) \), then we are done. Otherwise, suppose that \( \forall a,b,c
    \in \mathbb{Q} \) with \( a < b < c \), we do not have \( f\left(a\right) = f\left(b\right) =
    f\left(c\right) \). We show that such \( f \) must be unbounded on \( \left[0;1\right] \cap
    \mathbb{Q} \). Indeed, if this is not the case, then \( \exists K \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq0} \) such
    that \( f\left[\left[0;1\right] \cap \mathbb{Q}\right] \subset \llbracket -K;K \rrbracket \).
    Since \( \left[0;1\right] \cap \mathbb{Q} = \bigsqcup_{j \in \llbracket -K;K \rrbracket}
    \left(f|_{\left[0;1\right] \cap \mathbb{Q}}\right)^{-1}\left[\left\{j\right\}\right] \) is a
    finite union and \( \left[0;1\right] \cap \mathbb{Q} \) is countably infinite, there exists
    \( j \in \llbracket -K;K \rrbracket \) such that \( f^{-1}\left[\left\{j\right\}\right] \) is
    countably infinite. Pick pairwise distinct \( a,b,c \in f^{-1}\left[\left\{j\right\}\right] \)
    and, without loss of generality, \( a < b < c \). Then \( j = f\left(a\right) = f\left(b\right)
    = f\left(c\right) \), a contradiction to our assumptions on \( f \). Thus,
    \( f|_{\left[0;1\right] \cap \mathbb{Q}} \) is unbounded. In particular, there exists
    \( b \in \left]0;1\right[ \cap \mathbb{Q} \) such that \( f\left(0\right), f\left(1\right) \leq
    f\left(b\right) \). Take \( a = 0 \) and \( c = 1 \), then \( a < b < c \) and
    \( f\left(a\right), f\left(c\right) \leq f\left(b\right) \), as desired.
    \begin{remark}
        Notice that we did not use all the properties of
        \( \left\langle\mathbb{Q},<\right\rangle \); in particular, we did not use any property of
        its elements, nor the fact that its order \( < \) has neither a first nor a last element. In
        fact, one easily adapts the above proof to any partial strict
        \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_order}{dense order} \( \left\langle
        S,<_S\right\rangle \) with at least two \( <_S \)-comparable elements: any \( f : S \to
        \mathbb{Z} \) has the property that \( \exists a,b,c \in S \) such that \( a <_S b <_S c \)
        with \( f\left(a\right), f\left(c\right) \leq f\left(b\right) \). Again, we may assume, for
        the sake of contradiction, that \( \forall a,b,c \in S \) with \( a <_S b <_S c \),
        \( f\left(a\right) = f\left(b\right) = f\left(c\right) \) does not hold. Take two
        \( <_S \)-comparable elements \( a,c \in S \) with \( a <_S c \). By the axiom of countable
        choice, one easily obtains a countable \( <_S \)-chain \( \mathbf{x} : \mathbb{N}
        \hookrightarrow S \) such that for all \( i \in \mathbb{N}_{>0} \),
        \[
            a <_S \mathbf{x}\left(i+1\right) <_S \mathbf{x}\left(i\right) <_S
            \mathbf{x}\left(0\right) <_S c.
        \]
        Hence, for the same reason as before, \( f|_{\operatorname{ran}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)} \)
        is unbounded. In particular, there exists \( i \in \mathbb{N} \) such that
        \( f\left(a\right), f\left(c\right) \leq f\left(\mathbf{x}\left(i\right)\right) \). Then
        define \( b := \mathbf{x}\left(i\right) \in S \). We have \( a <_S b <_S c \), and
        \( f\left(a\right), f\left(c\right) \leq f\left(b\right) \), as desired.
    \end{remark}
\end{solution}
