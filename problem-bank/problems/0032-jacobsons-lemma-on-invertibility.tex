\begin{problem}
Let \( n \in \mathbb{N}_{>0} \) and \( A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n} \) be real matrices. Now suppose \( I_n - BA \) is invertible, where \( I_n \) is the identity matrix. Prove that \( I_n - AB \) is also invertible.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
Since \( I_n - AB \) is a square matrix, it suffices to show that its kernel is trivial. So let \( x \in \mathbb{R}^n \) with \( (I_n - AB)x = 0 \), i.e.\ \( x = ABx \). Put \( y = Bx \). Then
\[
(I_n - BA)y = Bx - BABx = B(x - ABx) = 0.
\]
As \( I_n - BA \) is invertible, \( y = 0 \). Hence \( x = ABx = Ay = 0 \). Therefore \( I_n - AB \) is injective, and by rank--nullity it is invertible.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Second solution (general rings).}
Let \( (R, +_R, \cdot_R, 0_R, 1_R) \) be any (possibly non-commutative) unital ring, let \(\textcolor{red}{a}, \textcolor{blue}{b} \in R\), we show that:
\[
1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \in R^{\times} \leftrightarrow 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \in R^{\times}.
\]
By symmetry in \( \textcolor{red}{a}, \textcolor{blue}{b} \in R \), we only need to prove one direction of the equivalence. So suppose \( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \in R^{\times} \) and put \( u = \left( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right)^{-1} \). We claim that \( 1_R + \textcolor{red}{a}u\textcolor{blue}{b} \) is the inverse of \( 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \). Using \( \textcolor{red}{a} - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} = \textcolor{red}{a}\left( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right) \) and \( \textcolor{blue}{b} - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} = \left( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right)\textcolor{blue}{b} \), we get
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left( 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \right)\left( 1_R + \textcolor{red}{a}u\textcolor{blue}{b} \right)
&= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \left( \textcolor{red}{a} - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right)u\textcolor{blue}{b}
= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \textcolor{red}{a}\left( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right)u\textcolor{blue}{b} \\
&= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} = 1_R, \\[4pt]
\left( 1_R + \textcolor{red}{a}u\textcolor{blue}{b} \right)\left( 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \right)
&= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \textcolor{red}{a}u\left( \textcolor{blue}{b} - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \right)
= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \textcolor{red}{a}u\left( 1_R - \textcolor{blue}{b}\textcolor{red}{a} \right)\textcolor{blue}{b} \\
&= 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} + \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} = 1_R.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence \( 1_R - \textcolor{red}{a}\textcolor{blue}{b} \in R^{\times} \). Apply this result to the non-commutative unital ring \( \left( \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}, +, \cdot, \mathbf{0}_{n \times n}, I_n \right) \).
\end{solution}
