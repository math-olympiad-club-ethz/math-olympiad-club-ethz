\begin{problem}
Does there exist a real $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ such that 
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0
\quad\text{and}\quad
A^2 + A^T = I_3,
\]
where $\operatorname{tr}(A)$ denotes the trace of $A$, $A^T$ is the transpose of $A$, and $I_3$ is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix?
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
The answer is no. Suppose that $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$ and $A^2 + A^T = I_3$. Taking the transpose, we have
\[
A = I_3 - \left(A^2\right)^T = I_3 - \left(A^T\right)^2 = I_3 - \left(I_3 - A^2\right)^2 = 2A^2 - A^4,
\]
so
\[
A^4 - 2A^2 + A = 0_3 .
\]
The roots of the polynomial $x^4 - 2x^2 + x = x(x-1)(x^2+x-1)$ are $0$, $1$, $\frac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2}$, so these numbers are the only possible eigenvalues of $A$; the corresponding eigenvalues of $A^2$ can be $0$, $1$, $\frac{3\mp\sqrt5}{2}$.

Since $\operatorname{tr}(A) = 0$, the sum of the eigenvalues of $A$ is $0$. Since
\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(A^2\right) = \operatorname{tr}\left(I_3 - A^T\right) = 3,
\]
the sum of the squares of the eigenvalues of $A$ is $3$. It is easy to check that these two conditions cannot be satisfied simultaneously.
\end{solution}
