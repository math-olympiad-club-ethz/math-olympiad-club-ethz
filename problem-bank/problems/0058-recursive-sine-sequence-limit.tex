\begin{problem}
Given the sequence $\{x_n\}_{n=0}^{+\infty}$, where $x_0 = 1$ and $x_{n+1} = \sin(x_n)+\frac{\pi}{2}-1$, prove that it converges, and find the limit.
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
$\sin x - x$ is decreasing between 0 and $\frac{\pi}{2}$ by a simple derivative test, so $\sin x_n - x_n \geq 1 - \frac{\pi}{2}$ (value at intermediate point vs. value at endpoint). This immediately implies $x_{n+1} \geq x_n$.\\
$\frac{\pi}{2}$ is an easy upper bound for the sequence, so it converges by the Monotone Convergence theorem. The limit must be a fixed point, the unique solution between 0 and $\frac{\pi}{2}$ being $\frac{\pi}{2}$, which must be our limit.
\end{solution}
