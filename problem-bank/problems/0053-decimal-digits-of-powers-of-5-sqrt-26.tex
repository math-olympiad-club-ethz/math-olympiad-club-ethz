\begin{problem}
    Prove that the decimal part of
    \[
        {\left(5 + \sqrt{26}\right)}^{n}
    \]
    begins with either \( n \) zeros or \( n \) nines for all positive integers \( n \).
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
    It is clear that \( {\left(5 + \sqrt{26}\right)}^{n} + {\left(5 - \sqrt{26}\right)}^{n} \) is an
    integer, whether by expanding the products and noting that all odd powers of \( \sqrt{26} \)
    cancel, or by using Galois theory - a sum of algebraic integers is an algebraic integer, the sum
    of all the roots is rational, and a rational algebraic integer is an integer.\\
    What remains is to show \( |{\left(5 - \sqrt{26}\right)}^{n}| < 10^{-n} \), or simply
    \( 5 - \sqrt{26} = \frac{1}{5 + \sqrt{26}} < \frac{1}{5+5} < \frac{1}{10} \).
    \begin{remark}
        These are called
        \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot%E2%80%93Vijayaraghavan_number}{Pisot–Vijayaraghavan numbers}.
    \end{remark}
\end{solution}
