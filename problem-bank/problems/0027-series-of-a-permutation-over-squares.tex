\begin{problem}
    Does there exist a bijective map \( \pi \colon \mathbb{N}_{>0} \to \mathbb{N}_{>0} \) such that
    \[
        \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} < \infty?
    \]
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
    \textit{Solution 1.}
    \\\\
    No. For a very quick and clever solution, if we let \( \pi \) be a permutation of
    \( \mathbb{N}_{>0} \) and let \( N \in \mathbb{N} \), we shall argue that
    \[
        \sum_{n=N+1}^{3N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} > \frac{1}{9}.
    \]
    In fact, of the \( 2N \) numbers \( \pi\left[\llbracket
    N+1;3N\rrbracket\right]=\left\{\pi\left(N+1\right), \ldots, \pi\left(3N\right)\right\} \), only
    \( N \) can be smaller than or equal to \( N \), so at least \( N \) of them must be strictly
    bigger than \( N \). Hence,
    \[
        \sum_{n=N+1}^{3N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} \geq \frac{1}{\left(3N\right)^2}
        \sum_{n=N+1}^{3N} \pi\left(n\right) \geq \frac{1}{9N^2} \cdot N \cdot N = \frac{1}{9}.
    \]
    The result follows directly because we have the infinite decomposition \( \mathbb{N}_{>0}=
    \bigsqcup_{N\in 3\mathbb{N}} \llbracket N+1;3N \rrbracket \).
    \\\\
    \textit{Alternative solutions.} There are two more solutions, both of which use the following
    fact:
    \\\\
    Let \( \pi \) be a permutation of \( \mathbb{N}^{*} \). Fix \( N \in \mathbb{N}^{*} \): the set
    of numbers \( \pi\left[\llbracket 1;N \rrbracket\right] = \left\{\pi\left(1\right), \ldots,
    \pi\left(N\right)\right\} \) is of size \( N \), i.e., the numbers are distinct positive
    integers. Thus, it is easy to prove\footnote{For the case \( N=1 \), take \( \iota_1 =
    \mathrm{id}_{\llbracket 1;1 \rrbracket} \). The condition holds vacuously as \( \llbracket 1;N-1
    \rrbracket = \varnothing \).
    \\\\
    Now assume the result holds for \( N \geq 1 \). We prove it for \( N+1 \). By the inductive
    hypothesis, there exists a permutation \( \iota_N \colon \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket
    \hookrightarrow \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \) such that
    \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i+1\right)\right) > \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i\right)\right) \) for all
    \( i \in \llbracket 1;N-1 \rrbracket \). If \( \pi\left(N+1\right) >
    \pi\left(\iota_N\left(N\right)\right) \), define \( \iota_{N+1} = \iota_N \cup \left\{\left(N+1,
    N+1\right)\right\} \). This extends \( \iota_N \) to \( \llbracket 1;N+1 \rrbracket \) while
    preserving the order, so the result holds. Else, by injectivity, equality is impossible, so
    \( \pi\left(N+1\right) < \pi\left(\iota_N\left(N\right)\right) \), and hence we can take \( k \)
    to be the smallest index in \( \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \) such that \( \pi\left(N+1\right) <
    \pi\left(\iota_N\left(k\right)\right) \). Define:
    \[
        \iota_{N+1} = \iota_N|_{\llbracket1;k-1\rrbracket} \cup \left\{\left(k, N+1\right)\right\}
        \cup \left\{\left(t+1, \iota_N\left(t\right)\right)\,\middle|\, t \in \llbracket k;N
        \rrbracket\right\}.
    \]
    This changes the value at \( k \) to \( N+1 \) and shifts the rest to take the preceding value.
    Clearly, \( \iota_{N+1} \) is a bijection, and \( \iota_{N+1}|_{\llbracket1;k-1\rrbracket} \)
    preserves the order. By choice of \( k \), \( \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(k\right)\right) =
    \pi\left(N+1\right) < \pi\left(\iota_N\left(k\right)\right) =
    \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(k+1\right)\right) \). Now if \( k > 1 \), then by minimality (and
    again by injectivity), we have \( \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(k\right)\right) =
    \pi\left(N+1\right) > \pi\left(\iota_N\left(k-1\right)\right) =
    \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(k-1\right)\right) \). In all cases,
    \( \iota_{N+1}|_{\llbracket1;k+1\rrbracket} \) preserves the order. For \( N \geq i > k \), we
    have \( \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(i+1\right)\right) = \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i\right)\right) >
    \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i-1\right)\right) = \pi\left(\iota_{N+1}\left(i\right)\right) \) by the
    inductive hypothesis. In total, \( \iota_{N+1} \) satisfies the required ordering. This
    concludes the induction step and hence the induction.} by induction over \( \mathbb{N}^{*} \)
    that there exists a permutation \( \iota_N \colon \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \hookrightarrow
    \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \) such that:
    \[
        \forall i \in \llbracket 1;N-1 \rrbracket,\quad \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i+1\right)\right) >
        \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i\right)\right).
    \]
    \textit{Solution 2.}
    \\\\
    Fix \( N \geq 1 \). From our proposition above, it follows that there is a permutation
    \( \iota_N \) of \( \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \) such that for all \( t \in \llbracket 1;N-1
    \rrbracket \), \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t+1\right)\right) >
    \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t\right)\right) \). In particular, since
    \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(1\right)\right) \geq 1 \), we get trivially by induction that for all
    \( t \in \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \), \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t\right)\right) \geq t \), so
    that:
    \[
        \sum_{i=1}^{N} \pi\left(i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \pi\left(\iota_N\left(i\right)\right) \geq
        \sum_{i=1}^{N} i = \frac{N\left(N+1\right)}{2},
    \]
    and this holds for all \( N \in \mathbb{N}^{*} \). Now we perform the very useful-to-know
    \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summation_by_parts}{Abel transformation} on the finite
    sequences \( \pi|_{\llbracket 1; N \rrbracket} \) and
    \( \left(\frac{1}{n^2}\right)_{1 \leq n \leq N} \) to obtain:
    \[
        \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} = \frac{1}{N^2} \left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}
        \pi\left(n\right)\right) + \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n} \pi\left(j\right)\right)
        \left( \frac{1}{n^2} - \frac{1}{\left(n+1\right)^2} \right)
    \]
    \[
        \geq \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \left( \frac{n\left(n+1\right)}{2} \right) \left( \frac{1}{n^2} -
        \frac{1}{\left(n+1\right)^2} \right) = \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \frac{2n+1}{2n\left(n+1\right)} \geq
        \sum_{n=1}^{N-1} \frac{1}{n+1} = \sum_{n=2}^{N} \frac{1}{n}.
    \]
    Thus,
    \[
        \liminf_{N \to +\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} \geq
        \liminf_{N \to +\infty} \sum_{n=2}^{N} \frac{1}{n} = +\infty.
    \]
    \textit{Solution 3.}
    \\\\
    Fix \( N \geq 1 \). Again, from our proposition, there is a permutation \( \iota_N \) of
    \( \llbracket 1;N \rrbracket \) such that for all \( t \in \llbracket 1;N-1 \rrbracket \),
    \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t+1\right)\right) > \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t\right)\right) \). We are in
    the following situation:
    \[
        \frac{1}{N^2} \leq \cdots \leq \frac{1}{1^2}
    \]
    \[
        \pi\left(\iota_N\left(1\right)\right) \leq \cdots \leq \pi\left(\iota_N\left(N\right)\right)
    \]
    By the very useful-to-know
    \href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rearrangement_inequality}{rearrangement inequality}, we
    obtain:
    \[
        \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} \geq \sum_{n=1}^{N}
        \frac{\pi\left(\iota_N\left(n\right)\right)}{n^2}.
    \]
    Since \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(1\right)\right) \geq 1 \), we get trivially by induction that
    \( \pi\left(\iota_N\left(t\right)\right) \geq t \), so that:
    \[
        \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(\iota_N\left(n\right)\right)}{n^2} \geq \sum_{n=1}^{N}
        \frac{n}{n^2} = \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n}.
    \]
    Thus,
    \[
        \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} \geq \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n}.
    \]
    In particular, as \( N \) was arbitrary, we get:
    \[
        \liminf_{N \to +\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{\pi\left(n\right)}{n^2} \geq
        \liminf_{N \to +\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \frac{1}{n} = +\infty.
    \]
\end{solution}
