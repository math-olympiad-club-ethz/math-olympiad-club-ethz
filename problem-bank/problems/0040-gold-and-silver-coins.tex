\begin{problem}
There is a pile of silver coins on a table. John holds two pieces of paper and performs the
following process: at each step, he can add one gold coin to the table and write the current number of silver
coins on one piece of paper, or remove one silver coin from the table and write down the current number
of gold coins on the other piece of paper. This process runs until no more silver coins remain on the table.
Show that at the end of the process, the sums of the numbers on both pieces of paper are equal.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
Let $s$ and $g$ be the number of silver and gold coins on the table at any given step, respectively. Let $A$ be the sum of the numbers written on the first piece of paper (which records silver coins) and $B$ be the sum of the numbers written on the second piece of paper (which records gold coins). 

We claim that $A - B = s \cdot g$ is an invariant of the process. 

Initially, there are $S_0$ silver coins and zero gold coins on the table, and both papers are blank. Thus, $s = S_0$, $g = 0$, $A = 0$, and $B = 0$. The difference of the sums is:
$$ A - B = 0 - 0 = 0 $$
And the product of the coins is:
$$ s \cdot g = S_0 \cdot 0 = 0 $$
So the base case $A - B = s \cdot g$ holds. Now we check how these values change under the two allowed operations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Add a gold coin:} The number of gold coins increases by $1$ ($g \to g+1$), and the current number of silver coins $s$ is added to the first paper ($A \to A+s$). $B$ and $s$ remain unchanged.
    The new difference of the papers is $(A + s) - B$. Using our inductive hypothesis $A - B = s \cdot g$, we get:
    $$ (A + s) - B = (A - B) + s = s \cdot g + s = s(g + 1) $$
    This exactly matches the new product of the coins, so the invariant is maintained.
    
    \item \textbf{Remove a silver coin:} The number of silver coins decreases by $1$ ($s \to s-1$), and the current number of gold coins $g$ is added to the second paper ($B \to B+g$). $A$ and $g$ remain unchanged.
    The new difference of the papers is $A - (B + g)$. Again, substituting $A - B = s \cdot g$, we get:
    $$ A - (B + g) = (A - B) - g = s \cdot g - g = g(s - 1) $$
    This exactly matches the new product of the coins, so the invariant is maintained.
\end{enumerate}

Since the relation $A - B = s \cdot g$ holds at the beginning and is preserved by every valid move, it holds throughout the entire game.

The process terminates when no more silver coins remain on the table, meaning the final number of silver coins is $s = 0$. Substituting this into our invariant yields:
$$ A - B = 0 \cdot g = 0 \implies A = B $$
Thus, at the end of the process, the sums of the numbers on both pieces of paper are exactly equal.
\end{solution}
