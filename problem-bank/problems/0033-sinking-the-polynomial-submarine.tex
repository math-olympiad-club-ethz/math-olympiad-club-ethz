\begin{problem}
    An enemy submarine is hidden somewhere along the infinite line \( \mathbb{R} \). It travels
    silently, and you know that its path is described by a fixed rational polynomial rule
    \( \sum_{i=0}^{n}c_iT^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\left[X\right] \), i.e. for each time \( t \in
    \mathbb{N} \), its position is given by
    \[
        x\left(t\right) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i t^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}.
    \]
    However, you do not know \( n \in \mathbb{N} \), nor the rational coefficients
    \( \left(c_i\right)_{i \in n} \in \mathbb{Q}^n \) that form this rational polynomial rule.
    \\\\
    Each unit of time \( t \in \mathbb{N}, starting at t=0 \), you are allowed to launch a single
    torpedo at any chosen rational position. If the submarine is at that position at that time, it
    is struck and sinks.
    \\\\
    Devise a strategy — a countable sequence of torpedo launches — such that, regardless of the
    submarine's unknown polynomial rule, you will \textbf{eventually} hit it.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
    The submarine's position at time \( t \in \mathbb{N} \) is given by an unknown polynomial
    \( x\left(t\right) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i t^{i} \), where the degree \( n \) and coefficients
    \( \mathbf{c} = \left(c_0, \dots, c_n\right) \in \mathbb{Q}^{n+1} \) are fixed but unknown. We
    seek a sequence of torpedo launches \( f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q} \) such that
    \( f\left(\tilde{t}\right) = x\left(\tilde{t}\right) \) for some time \( \tilde{t} \in
    \mathbb{N} \).

    To ensure we eventually hit the submarine constructively (without relying on the Axiom of
    Choice), we explicitly define a surjection \( g: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega} \) to
    systematically enumerate every possible finite sequence of rational coefficients.

    \textit{Step 1: Bijection between \( \mathbb{N} \) and \( \mathbb{Z} \)} \\
    Define \( s: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z} \) by:
    \[
        s\left(n\right) = \left(-1\right)^n \left\lfloor \frac{n + 1}{2} \right\rfloor.
    \]

    \textit{Step 2: Bijection between \( \mathbb{Z} \) and \( \mathbb{Q} \)} \\
    Every positive integer \( m \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \) has a unique prime factorization \( m =
    p_1^{a_1} p_2^{a_2} \cdots \), where \( p_i \) is the \( i \)-th prime. We define \( h_+:
    \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \to \mathbb{Q}_{>0} \) by mapping the exponents through \( s \):
    \[
        h_+\left(m\right) = p_1^{s\left(a_1\right)} p_2^{s\left(a_2\right)} \cdots
    \]
    This extends to a bijection \( h: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q} \) by setting \( h\left(0\right) =
    0 \), \( h\left(m\right) = h_+\left(m\right) \) for \( m > 0 \), and \( h\left(m\right) =
    -h_+\left(-m\right) \) for \( m < 0 \). Composing these gives a constructive bijection
    \( \phi = h \circ s: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q} \).

    \textit{Step 3: Surjection from \( \mathbb{N} \) to \( \mathbb{N}^{<\omega} \)} \\
    We map finite sequences of natural numbers to \( \mathbb{N} \) using a prime encoding function.
    Define \( E: \mathbb{N}^{<\omega} \to \mathbb{N} \) by \( E\left(\emptyset\right) = 0 \) and
    \[
        E\left(a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{k-1}\right) = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} p_i^{a_i + 1} - 1.
    \]
    Because \( E \) is injective, its inverse \( E^{-1} \) is well-defined on its image. We can
    construct a surjection \( \psi: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}^{<\omega} \) by setting
    \( \psi\left(m\right) = E^{-1}\left(m\right) \) if \( m \) is in the image of \( E \), and
    \( \psi\left(m\right) = \emptyset \) otherwise.

    \textit{Step 4: The Strategy} \\
    We combine these to form our surjection \( g: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega} \). For each
    time \( t \in \mathbb{N} \), we compute \( \psi\left(t\right) = \left(a_0, \dots, a_{k-1}\right)
    \in \mathbb{N}^{<\omega} \), and then apply \( \phi \) element-wise to get \( g\left(t\right) =
    \left(\phi\left(a_0\right), \dots, \phi\left(a_{k-1}\right)\right) \in \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega} \).

    At time \( t \), we assume \( g\left(t\right) \) represents the submarine's true coefficients.
    Let \( g\left(t\right) = \left(c'_0, \dots, c'_{k-1}\right) \). We launch our torpedo at
    position:
    \[
        f\left(t\right) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} c'_i t^{i}.
    \]
    Because \( g \) is surjective, the submarine's true coefficient tuple \( \mathbf{c} \) must
    appear in our enumeration at some time \( \tilde{t} \) (i.e., \( g\left(\tilde{t}\right) =
    \mathbf{c} \)). At that exact time \( \tilde{t} \), our torpedo's computed position
    \( f\left(\tilde{t}\right) \) will perfectly evaluate the submarine's true polynomial
    \( x\left(\tilde{t}\right) \), guaranteeing a hit.
\end{solution}
