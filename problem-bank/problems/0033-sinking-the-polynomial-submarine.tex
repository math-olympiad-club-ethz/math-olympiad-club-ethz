\begin{problem}
An enemy submarine is hidden somewhere along the infinite line $\mathbb{R}$. It travels silently, and you know that its path is described by a fixed rational polynomial rule \(\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_iT^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]\), i.e. for each time \(t \in \mathbb{N}\), its position is given by
\[
x(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i t^{i}\in\mathbb{Q}.
\]
However, you do not know \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), nor the rational coefficients \((c_i)_{i \in n} \in \mathbb{Q}^n\) that form this rational polynomial rule.
\\\\
Each unit of time \(t \in \mathbb{N}, starting at t=0\), you are allowed to launch a single torpedo at any chosen rational position. If the submarine is at that position at that time, it is struck and sinks.
\\\\
Devise a strategy — a countable sequence of torpedo launches — such that, regardless of the submarine's unknown polynomial rule, you will \textbf{eventually} hit it.
\end{problem}

\begin{solution}
The submarine's position at time \(t \in \mathbb{N}\) is given by an unknown polynomial \(x(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} c_i t^{i}\), where the degree \(n\) and coefficients \(\mathbf{c} = (c_0, \dots, c_n) \in \mathbb{Q}^{n+1}\) are fixed but unknown. We seek a sequence of torpedo launches \(f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}\) such that \(f(\tilde{t}) = x(\tilde{t})\) for some time \(\tilde{t} \in \mathbb{N}\).

To ensure we eventually hit the submarine constructively (without relying on the Axiom of Choice), we explicitly define a surjection \(g: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega}\) to systematically enumerate every possible finite sequence of rational coefficients. 

\textbf{Step 1: Bijection between \(\mathbb{N}\) and \(\mathbb{Z}\)} \\
Define \(s: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}\) by:
\[
s(n) = (-1)^n \left\lfloor \frac{n + 1}{2} \right\rfloor.
\]

\textbf{Step 2: Bijection between \(\mathbb{Z}\) and \(\mathbb{Q}\)} \\
Every positive integer \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}\) has a unique prime factorization \(m = p_1^{a_1} p_2^{a_2} \cdots\), where \(p_i\) is the \(i\)-th prime. We define \(h_+: \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \to \mathbb{Q}_{>0}\) by mapping the exponents through \(s\):
\[
h_+(m) = p_1^{s(a_1)} p_2^{s(a_2)} \cdots
\]
This extends to a bijection \(h: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Q}\) by setting \(h(0) = 0\), \(h(m) = h_+(m)\) for \(m > 0\), and \(h(m) = -h_+(-m)\) for \(m < 0\). Composing these gives a constructive bijection \(\phi = h \circ s: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}\).

\textbf{Step 3: Surjection from \(\mathbb{N}\) to \(\mathbb{N}^{<\omega}\)} \\
We map finite sequences of natural numbers to \(\mathbb{N}\) using a prime encoding function. Define \(E: \mathbb{N}^{<\omega} \to \mathbb{N}\) by \(E(\emptyset) = 0\) and
\[
E(a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{k-1}) = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} p_i^{a_i + 1} - 1.
\]
Because \(E\) is injective, its inverse \(E^{-1}\) is well-defined on its image. We can construct a surjection \(\psi: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}^{<\omega}\) by setting \(\psi(m) = E^{-1}(m)\) if \(m\) is in the image of \(E\), and \(\psi(m) = \emptyset\) otherwise.

\textbf{Step 4: The Strategy} \\
We combine these to form our surjection \(g: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega}\). For each time \(t \in \mathbb{N}\), we compute \(\psi(t) = (a_0, \dots, a_{k-1}) \in \mathbb{N}^{<\omega}\), and then apply \(\phi\) element-wise to get \(g(t) = (\phi(a_0), \dots, \phi(a_{k-1})) \in \mathbb{Q}^{<\omega}\).

At time \(t\), we assume \(g(t)\) represents the submarine's true coefficients. Let \(g(t) = (c'_0, \dots, c'_{k-1})\). We launch our torpedo at position:
\[
f(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} c'_i t^{i}.
\]
Because \(g\) is surjective, the submarine's true coefficient tuple \(\mathbf{c}\) must appear in our enumeration at some time \(\tilde{t}\) (i.e., \(g(\tilde{t}) = \mathbf{c}\)). At that exact time \(\tilde{t}\), our torpedo's computed position \(f(\tilde{t})\) will perfectly evaluate the submarine's true polynomial \(x(\tilde{t})\), guaranteeing a hit.
\end{solution}
